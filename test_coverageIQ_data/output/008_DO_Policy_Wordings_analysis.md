## DO-002 — Insured vs. Insured / Bankruptcy Carve-Back
Status: PASS
Confidence: High

Finding: An Insured vs. Insured exclusion is identified at Section 3.4, containing a qualifying bankruptcy-related carve-back for claims brought by a liquidator, receiver, or administrative receiver acting without the solicitation, assistance, or participation of any insured or the company.
Evidence (IVI Provision): "3.4 which is directly or indirectly arising out of, based upon or attributable to claims brought by or on behalf of any insured or the company; provided, however, that this exclusion shall not apply to:" (Page 5)
Evidence (Carve-Back): "3.4.4. any claim brought or maintained by a liquidator, receiver or administrative receiver either directly or derivatively on behalf of the company without the solicitation, assistance or participation of any insured or the company;" (Page 5)
Reasoning: This policy is governed by Indian law (General Provision 5.13), so it uses "liquidator" and "administrative receiver" — the operative Indian company-law/insolvency terminology — rather than the U.S.-style phrase "bankruptcy trustee." These map directly onto the rule's own listed carve-back categories (Receiver/Receivership; Liquidator/Conservator/Rehabilitator) and function identically: preserving coverage for claims brought by a court-appointed representative standing in the company's shoes, independent of insured involvement. The exclusion also separately carves back shareholder derivative actions (3.4.3) and former-director/officer claims after a 12-month gap (3.4.5), but the bankruptcy-type carve-back at 3.4.4 is what satisfies this rule.

## DO-004 — BI/PD Exclusion / Emotional Distress Carve-Back
Status: PASS
Confidence: High

Finding: A Bodily Injury / Property Damage exclusion is identified at Section 3.8, containing an emotional distress carve-back scoped to employment practice claims.
Evidence: "3.8 for bodily injury, sickness, disease, death or emotional distress of any person, or damage to or destruction of any tangible property, including loss of use thereof; provided, however, that any claim for emotional distress shall not be excluded with respect to an employment practice claim;" (Page 6)
Reasoning: The carve-back is not a blanket exception — it restores emotional distress coverage only "with respect to an employment practice claim" (as defined at Section 2.8: wrongful termination, harassment, discrimination, etc.). This is exactly the narrower, employment-scoped pattern the rule anticipates as a passing result rather than requiring an unqualified exception.
