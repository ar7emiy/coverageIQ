Do not automatically treat references to independent contractors as proof of Third-Party EPLI.
Independent contractors may be treated as insured persons, employees, claimants, or third parties depending on the form.

⸻

A carrier does not need to use the phrase “Third-Party EPLI.”
For example, a policy could provide coverage for:

If Third-Party EPLI is affirmatively provided, the rule passes unless another provision materially removes that coverage.
If a separate Third-Party EPLI sublimit exists, CoverageIQ should extract and display it, but should not fail the rule based solely on the amount unless a future administrator-defined threshold is established.

EPLI-004 — Independent Contractor Coverage
Coverage Module: Employment Practices Liability (EPLI)
Rule Type: Coverage Optimization
Rule Name: Independent Contractor Coverage
Objective
Determine whether the EPLI policy or quote provides coverage involving independent contractors, primarily by reviewing the definition of Employee and related insured-person definitions.
CoverageIQ must then review the entire document for any exclusion, endorsement, or limitation that removes or restricts coverage involving independent contractors.
The preferred result is:
Independent contractors are included within the applicable EPLI coverage and no subsequent exclusion or endorsement materially removes that coverage.


⸻


Step 1 — Review the Definition of Employee
CoverageIQ should first locate and analyze the policy’s definition of:
Employee
Employees
Insured Person
Individual Insured
Worker
Employment Practices Insured
Other materially equivalent defined terms governing who qualifies for EPLI coverage
The Employee definition should be the primary starting point.
Within these definitions, search semantically for concepts including:
Independent Contractor
Independent Contractors
Contract Worker
Contract Employee
Contractor
Freelance Worker
Freelancer
Temporary Worker
Temporary Employee
Leased Employee
Leased Worker
Similar terminology describing individuals providing services who may not qualify as traditional employees
CoverageIQ should evaluate the substance of the definition, rather than requiring the exact phrase “independent contractor.”


⸻


Step 2 — Determine Whether Independent Contractors Are Included
CoverageIQ must determine whether independent contractors or materially equivalent workers are affirmatively included within the applicable definition.
Example:
“Employee means any past, present or future employee, including any part-time, seasonal, temporary, leased or independent contractor…”
This constitutes strong affirmative evidence.
If independent contractors are not identified within the Employee definition, CoverageIQ must continue reviewing other applicable definitions, insuring agreements, and endorsements before concluding that coverage is absent.
An endorsement may separately add independent contractors even when they do not appear within the base definition.


⸻


Step 3 — Search for Endorsements Expanding Coverage
If independent contractors are not included within the base Employee definition, search the entire document for an endorsement or other provision that affirmatively extends EPLI coverage to independent contractors.
Search for concepts including:
Independent Contractor Coverage
Independent Contractors Included
Independent Contractors as Employees
Independent Contractor Extension
Definition of Employee Amended
Definition of Employee Expanded
Amend Definition of Employee
Contractor Coverage
Similar language extending EPLI protection to independent contractors
If an applicable endorsement affirmatively adds independent contractors, treat them as included for purposes of this rule.


⸻


Step 4 — Search for Exclusions or Endorsements Removing Coverage
This step is mandatory even if independent contractors are included within the base Employee definition.
CoverageIQ must review the entire policy for any exclusion, endorsement, or limitation that subsequently removes or restricts coverage involving independent contractors.
Search semantically for concepts including:
Independent Contractor Exclusion
Independent Contractors Exclusion
Contractor Exclusion
Claims involving Independent Contractors
Claims brought by Independent Contractors
Claims arising from Independent Contractors
Independent Contractor Limitation
Misclassification of Independent Contractors
Worker Classification
Employment Status
Similar provisions restricting coverage involving contract workers
CoverageIQ must determine the actual effect of the provision rather than flagging the mere appearance of these terms.


⸻


Step 5 — Reconcile Base Form and Endorsements
CoverageIQ must evaluate the final operative coverage, not simply the most favorable language it encounters.
For example:
Base Form:
Employee includes independent contractors.
Later Endorsement:
Independent Contractor Exclusion…
CoverageIQ must recognize that the endorsement may modify or remove the favorable base-form coverage.
Conversely:
Base Form:
Employee does not include independent contractors.
Later Endorsement:
Definition of Employee amended to include independent contractors.
CoverageIQ should recognize that the endorsement may add the desired coverage.
Applicable endorsements must be evaluated together with the base form before assigning a final status.


⸻

No operative exclusion or limitation materially removes that coverage,
return:

Provide evidence supporting the inclusion.

Provide evidence from the existing Employee definition and any other relevant provisions.

If independent contractors initially appear to be included, but an applicable exclusion, endorsement, or limitation subsequently removes or materially restricts that coverage:

⚠️ OPTIMIZATION

Consider requesting removal of the exclusion or limitation restricting EPLI coverage for independent contractors.

The provision that removes or restricts the coverage.

Whether another defined term provides equivalent coverage.

The mere appearance of “independent contractor” within an exclusion does not automatically mean coverage has been removed.
